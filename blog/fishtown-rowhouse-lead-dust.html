Fishtown: rowhouse living and the dust old windows make

Fishtown's rowhouse blocks were built for workers who walked to the mills and factories nearby. Those houses have been painted and repainted for over a century, and the windows that stick and rub are often where a lead question starts.

Rowhouses share their age, not their condition

The Encyclopedia of Greater Philadelphia describes Fishtown among the industrial neighborhoods whose row house blocks housed factory workers. That shared history makes lead paint a fair assumption to check, but only a test answers it for a specific house. Your neighbor's report is not your report.

Name the dust before naming the fix

Where does it collect: the sill, the trough, the floor under the window? Does it return within days of cleaning? Those observations tell a professional whether the question is a rubbing sash, a failing exterior coating or both. Describe the pattern instead of ordering a repaint.

Window work has more than one version

Repair with lead-safe practices, component replacement, enclosure and abatement are different projects with different documentation. Ask the firm to name the project type and its qualifications. A replacement sales pitch should not decide the hazard-control method.

Do not make dust while investigating

Avoid sanding or dry scraping suspect paint to see what is underneath. EPA's renovation guidance explains why paid paint disturbance in applicable older housing requires lead-safe practices. Keep children away from flaking areas until a professional has looked.

A useful first request

Send the approximate construction date, which windows or rooms are involved, photos of the damage and any earlier lead report. Ask what evaluation comes first and what documentation will show the work is done. A rowhouse block can look uniform from the street; the scope should still be written for your house.

Common questions

Do rowhouse neighbors share lead reports?

No. Rowhouses share their age, not their condition. Only a test answers the question for a specific house, so your neighbor's report is not yours.

What is friction dust?

Fine dust created when painted parts rub, like a sash moving in its frame. It can collect in window troughs and on sills without dramatic peeling paint.
