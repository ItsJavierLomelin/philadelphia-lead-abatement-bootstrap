Chestnut Hill: renovating a big old house without a lead surprise

Chestnut Hill's large stone and frame houses grew with the railroad in the 1800s, and many are being renovated again. The safest remodel in a house that old starts by learning which painted surfaces the project will disturb.

The railroad built the Hill's housing

The Encyclopedia of Greater Philadelphia traces northwest Philadelphia's growth, including the 1854 railroad that connected Chestnut Hill to the city. The houses that followed have been updated many times, and each update can hide original painted components under newer finishes.

Sequence testing before demolition

Walls open fast in a remodel, and painted layers come apart faster. Test the surfaces the project will touch before sanding, scraping or demolition changes their condition. Bring the construction date, earlier reports and the project list to the inspection.

Renovation and abatement are different projects

EPA's renovation rule covers paid work that disturbs paint in applicable older housing; abatement permanently eliminates documented hazards. A kitchen remodel is not automatically abatement. Ask which project yours is and which credentials it needs.

Gardens and soil deserve a question too

Decades of exterior paint can flake toward the drip line. If bare soil sits near play areas or garden beds, ask a risk assessor whether soil sampling makes sense. Paint chips in the yard do not establish the laboratory concentration.

What a good scope looks like

Surfaces and rooms named, containment described, occupant protection arranged, cleanup specified and a final check agreed in advance. Keep it all in writing with the reports. A smooth finish is not proof the dust question was answered.

Common questions

When in a remodel should testing happen?

Before demolition. Bring the construction date, earlier reports and the project list to the inspection so the report maps to the components the work will disturb.

Is my renovation automatically abatement?

No. A remodel that disturbs paint needs lead-safe practices; abatement permanently eliminates documented hazards. The proposal should say which project it is.
