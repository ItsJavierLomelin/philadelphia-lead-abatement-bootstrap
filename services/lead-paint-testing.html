Lead Paint Testing and Inspection for Philadelphia Homes

Is that peeling paint actually lead? Color, texture and the age of a house cannot answer that question. A lead-based paint inspection identifies lead paint and its location. It is the right starting point when your main question is what materials you are about to disturb.

What testing can tell you

An inspector may use an XRF instrument or laboratory analysis of paint samples. Ask which surfaces will be tested and whether the written report identifies components by room. A quick consumer swab is not a substitute for a documented professional inspection when a regulated report is needed.

Inspection or risk assessment?

An inspection locates lead paint. A risk assessment looks for current hazards and recommends controls. EPA explains why these are different services. If paint chips, window dust or bare soil are the concern, describe those conditions rather than requesting a paint test alone.

Prepare for the visit

Gather the construction date, any previous lead reports and a list of planned work. Mention doors, stair rails, porch surfaces and original window sashes. Testing should happen before sanding, scraping or demolition changes the condition of those surfaces.

Common questions

Who can perform a lead paint inspection in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania certifies lead occupations through the Department of Labor and Industry. Ask for the credential that matches the task before you schedule.

Is a general home inspection the same as a lead inspection?

No. A general home inspection does not identify lead paint surface by surface. A lead-based paint inspection uses XRF or laboratory analysis and produces a room-by-room record.

Do I need testing before a small remodel?

If the house predates 1978 and the work disturbs paint, identify the coatings first. Testing should happen before sanding, scraping or demolition changes the condition of those surfaces.
