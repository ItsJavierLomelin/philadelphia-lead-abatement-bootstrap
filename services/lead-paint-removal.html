Lead Paint Removal and Abatement in Philadelphia, PA

Removing lead paint is not a normal repainting job. The work can create contaminated dust if it is done without the right controls. Start with the inspection or risk-assessment report so the abatement scope targets documented hazards.

Removal is one option, not the only option

EPA describes abatement as permanent hazard elimination. Depending on the surface and its condition, a plan may use component replacement, enclosure, approved encapsulation or removal. Compare these choices with a qualified professional rather than assuming every painted surface must be stripped.

What belongs in the scope

Ask for the surfaces and rooms included, the containment plan, how occupants will be protected, cleanup procedures and the clearance arrangements. Discuss whether people or pets must leave the work area and who handles contaminated waste. Do not accept a verbal promise that ordinary painting will permanently solve a documented hazard.

Before work starts

Verify Pennsylvania credentials: the Department of Labor and Industry certifies lead abatement contractors and requires a certified supervisor behind each firm. Review any building permits or Philadelphia Historical Commission approvals separately. Avoid dry sanding or scraping suspect paint yourself while waiting. A price depends on the findings, access and chosen method, not a flat cost per house.

Common questions

Does every lead-painted surface have to be stripped?

No. Depending on the surface and its condition, a plan may use component replacement, enclosure, approved encapsulation or removal. Compare the options before assuming everything comes off.

Can I scrape the paint myself while I wait?

EPA advises against removing lead-based paint yourself. Avoid dry sanding or scraping suspect paint, and use trained, certified people for work that disturbs it.

What should a written abatement scope include?

The surfaces and rooms included, the containment plan, occupant protection, cleanup procedures and the clearance arrangements, plus who handles contaminated waste.
