Lead-Safe Window and Door Replacement in Philadelphia

A sticking painted window can grind old coatings into dust each time it moves. If you are searching for "dust on window sills" or "lead paint on old windows," the useful question is whether the moving component is a continuing hazard.

Replacing the right components

A plan may involve a sash, a full window assembly or another painted component. Ask what remains, how surrounding trim is protected and whether the replacement is part of an abatement project or a renovation. The certification and final documentation requirements depend on that distinction.

Historic homes need two conversations

Lead safety and preservation approvals solve different problems. For a designated historic property, discuss exterior changes with the Philadelphia Historical Commission before ordering replacements. A lead assessment does not grant permission to remove original architectural features.

Dust control is part of the work

EPA explains lead-safe renovation requirements for paid work in applicable pre-1978 housing and child-occupied facilities. Verify the firm's qualifications for the actual scope and ask how demolition dust, cleanup and the final check will be handled.

Common questions

Will replacing the windows solve my dust problem?

It removes the rubbing components that make the dust. A clearance check afterward confirms whether dust remains in the work area.

My house is a designated historic property. Can I replace the windows?

Discuss exterior changes with the Philadelphia Historical Commission before ordering. A lead assessment does not grant permission to remove original architectural features.

Is window replacement abatement or renovation?

It depends on the purpose of the project. The certification and final documentation requirements follow that distinction, so ask which one your project is.
