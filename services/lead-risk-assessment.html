Lead Risk Assessment for Philadelphia Homes and Rentals

Dust keeps returning to the window sill. Paint is flaking near a toddler's play area. A risk assessment starts with the condition of the home and the ways someone could encounter lead, then turns findings into a plan.

A report that connects the dots

A certified risk assessor evaluates deteriorated paint and may collect dust or soil samples. The report should describe hazards, their locations and options for dealing with them. Tell the assessor about recent renovations, accessible exterior soil and rooms used by children.

Choose the right professional

Pennsylvania certifies lead occupations through the Department of Labor and Industry, and the credential for an inspector, a risk assessor and an abatement firm are not the same. Verify current certification for the particular task. An EPA renovation certificate is not interchangeable with Pennsylvania abatement or risk-assessment qualifications.

A health result is not a building diagnosis

If a child has an elevated blood lead result, follow the healthcare provider's advice and contact the local health department. A home assessment can investigate environmental sources, but this website cannot diagnose exposure or determine medical treatment. Do not assume paint is the only possible source.

Common questions

Who can perform a lead risk assessment in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania distinguishes inspectors, risk assessors and abatement firms, and each task has its own certification through the Department of Labor and Industry. Verify the current certification for the specific work before you hire.

What does the report give me?

The report describes the hazards found, their locations and the options for controlling them, so the work that follows targets documented conditions rather than guesses.

A child's test came back high. Is this the right step?

Follow the healthcare provider's advice and contact the local health department first. A home assessment can investigate environmental sources, but it cannot diagnose exposure or replace medical guidance.
