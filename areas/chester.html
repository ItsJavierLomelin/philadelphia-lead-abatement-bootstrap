Lead Paint Abatement in Chester, PA

Chester was settled in the 1600s and filled with workers after the Civil War, so its housing ranges from nineteenth-century rows to early twentieth-century twins. Almost all of it predates the 1978 lead paint ban.

Shipyards, textiles and very old streets

The Encyclopedia of Greater Philadelphia describes Chester's growth after the Civil War, when John Roach's iron shipyards and the textile industry drew Irish, Polish, Italian and African-American workers to the waterfront city.

Rapid industrial growth meant fast-built worker housing, painted and repainted ever since. Flaking exterior trim and deteriorated porch surfaces are common, and scraping them without controls can spread contaminated dust.

What that means for lead

Chester's housing carries layers of that industrial history. A porch or window repair can cross from cosmetic work into paint disturbance quickly, so evaluation belongs before scraping, not after.

Keep any city notice or housing-program letter with the project file. If exterior paint has flaked near the foundation for years, describe the bare ground below it and whether children use that space, so an assessor can decide whether soil sampling belongs in the investigation.

Common questions

The porch paint is peeling badly. Can I just scrape and repaint it?

EPA advises against removing lead-based paint yourself, and dry scraping is the fastest way to spread contaminated dust. Have the paint tested first; if it is lead, trained and certified people should handle the work.

Is bare soil near the house a lead concern?

It can be, where years of exterior paint have flaked near the foundation. A risk assessor can decide whether soil sampling makes sense for the spots children or pets use.
