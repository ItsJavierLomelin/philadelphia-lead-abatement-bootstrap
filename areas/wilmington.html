Lead Paint Abatement in Wilmington, DE

Wilmington's rowhouse neighborhoods grew with milling, shipbuilding and manufacturing along the Brandywine and Christina. Housing that old makes lead paint a fair assumption until testing says otherwise.

From Fort Christina to factory rows

The Encyclopedia of Greater Philadelphia traces the city from the 1638 Swedish settlement at Fort Christina through its years as a milling center whose Brandywine Superfine flour reached markets across the colonies and Europe.

Later industrial booms filled the city with worker housing. Those homes have been painted many times, and deteriorating coatings on porches, trim and windows are the usual starting point for a lead conversation.

What that means for lead

Wilmington's older rowhouse neighborhoods have seen long cycles of repair, vacancy and renewal. Flaking exterior paint and deteriorated trim are common triggers for a lead conversation.

Delaware's lead program and rental requirements are separate from Pennsylvania's. State the property's situation plainly when you call: building age, who lives there and what prompted the question. That keeps the recommended service tied to the actual requirement.

Common questions

Do Pennsylvania's lead rules cover Wilmington?

No. Wilmington is in Delaware, which has its own lead program and rental requirements. State the property location when you call so the recommended service matches the right state's rules.

The house looks freshly painted. Could it still have lead?

Yes. New paint can cover old lead paint without removing it. Only testing establishes what is in the layers underneath.
