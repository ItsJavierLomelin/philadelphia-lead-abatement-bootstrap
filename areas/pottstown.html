An iron town on the Schuylkill

The Pottstown Historical Society traces the borough to founder John Potts and preserves Pottsgrove Manor, the 1752 home from the town's ironmaking years.

Worker housing from that era was built fast and painted often. Peeling surfaces, rubbed window edges and flaking porch ceilings are everyday conditions in Pottstown's older blocks, and each is a testing question before it is a painting question.

What that means for lead

Pottstown's iron-era growth left blocks of older worker housing along the Schuylkill. Paint from many decades sits in layers on those surfaces, and a peeling window or porch is a reason to test, not scrape.

Describe what you see and where: the room, the component and how long it has been deteriorating. A photograph shows condition but not lead content, so let a test answer that question before work starts.

Common questions

Paint is peeling on several windows. Do I need the whole house stripped?

Not necessarily. An inspection or assessment shows which components actually carry lead and which are sound, so the scope follows the findings rather than a blanket assumption.

Is dust on the porch floor a lead concern?

It can be where paint above is failing. Describe where the dust collects and how often it returns, and ask whether dust sampling belongs in the evaluation.
